1The style in your reference video

"Ember Ghost" painterly boil animation
Also called: frame-by-frame painted animation · paint-on-glass look · "boiling" brush animation · expressionist digital oil painting shot "on threes" (9 fps)
🎨 Every frame repainted

Each frame is painted again, so the brush strokes jitter and "boil" all the time. Nothing is ever perfectly still.

⏱ 9 frames per second

Your file really is 9 fps. That low rate gives the hand-made stutter, so this film is painted at 9 fps too.

🔥 Ember body

The figure is painted in cream and white highlights over an orange and red underpainting, like a body made of fire. It has no real face, just a pale head shape.

🌑 Charcoal swirl

The background is near-black with big grey brush swirls, like a wall smeared with charcoal. It is the only "set".

✨ Confetti flecks

Small dashes of red, green, purple and yellow float in the air: the "world alive" layer.

🚶 One figure walking

A single glowing figure walks toward the camera or across it. The camera barely moves; the paint does the moving.

3Cinematic rules used (from your AI Film rules)

🎥 Camera has a job

A slow push-in while he hides it, a pull-back to reveal the family, a steady track while he chases them, and a slow push-in on the collapse.

🌫 Foreground layer

A soft dark brush smear passes close to the lens, so the frame feels filmed, not flat.

🔥 One light

His own ember is the only light. It lights the ground under his feet and grows or dies with his mood.

⏱ Every second has a job

Timed beats, real-time speed, no slow motion. The sadness comes from his posture, never from slowing time down.

🧍 Physical acting

Shoulders, back, head and step length tell the story: rounded and short when he's hiding it, straight and quick when he's chasing, knees giving way when they vanish.

🔊 Sound tells it too

Heavy slow steps, a warm chord when they appear, fast steps, then a hard cut to wind when they vanish.
