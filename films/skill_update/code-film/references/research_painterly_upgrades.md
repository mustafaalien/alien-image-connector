# Repaint the ember ghost like real glass

The biggest quality jump comes from three things, not from a new AI tool. First, paint the frames the way paint-on-glass and *Loving Vincent* artists did: keep one canvas, and repaint only the parts that move. Second, give the ghost real human motion by filming yourself acting the six beats on an iPhone. Third, light the red heart with real film-style glow, so it spills onto the paint around it. All three can be built by Claude in this container, with free tools, in roughly two to four working days of agent time plus about 30 minutes of filming from you. A full 30-second, 270-frame render should take about 10–20 minutes. Painted films earn their look by carrying paint over from frame to frame; Loving Vincent's canvases were repainted an average of 76 times ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2017/10/loving-vincent-head-painting-animating-van-gogh)). The current "boil", where every frame is redrawn from scratch, is what reads as computer-made. A careful sound mix, a colour plan and handwritten text then finish the job for almost no extra render time. The main risks are licences on some free motion data, and the software graphics in this container being slow for heavy filters. Both have clear workarounds, set out below.

Options that were looked at and placed lower: oil-paint filters such as the anisotropic Kuwahara filter (good on the background only, slow here); downloaded mocap libraries (fast, but the most emotional set is non-commercial); phone mocap apps such as Move One or Rokoko Vision (cleaner 3D, but you have to run them); a Blender mannequin pipeline (most control, most of your time); and EbSynth style transfer (not needed for a fully code-made film).

**Recommended combined pipeline in one sentence:** you film one continuous side-view take of the six beats. Claude turns it into a 9 fps skeleton, adds procedural follow-through, and paints each frame with body-anchored bristle strokes on a persistent canvas over a held, slowly boiling swirl plate. A WebGL pass adds HDR heart glow, rays and light wrap; ffmpeg grades, grains and encodes; and a synthesised heartbeat score is mixed in sync. The sections below explain why, and the last section gives the technical build.

## Painted films change the canvas instead of redrawing it

All three reference looks share one mechanism. In paint-on-glass, slow-drying oil (or gouache mixed with glycerine) is pushed around on glass ([Wikipedia mirror](https://kiwix.lema.org/wikipedia_en_all_mini_2024-02/A/Paint-on-glass_animation)). After each photograph the glass is partly wiped and changed, so each frame is a modified version of the last ([cypaint](https://cypaint.com/article/who-is-the-animator-famous-for-painting-on-glass)). Aleksandr Petrov paints with his fingertips and palm on a light table ([meer.com](https://www.meer.com/en/34734-my-love)). His *The Old Man and the Sea* took **29,000 frames and about 2.5 years** ([AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/old-man-and-sea-hands-above-rest)). *Loving Vincent* worked the same way at scale. It started each of its 853 shots from a painted keyframe and painted directly over it for every following frame ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/how-creators-loving-vincent-brought-first-fully-painted-animated-film-life-180968210/)). Only the moving parts, "a person's head and shoulders", were scraped off and repainted, **12 times for every second** ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/culture/film-tv/article/2118626/loving-vincent-how-van-goghs-life-was-subject-worlds-first-animated)). Brush texture, leftover paint and the static background therefore carry over between frames, and only the motion boils.

That is the upgrade the ember ghost is missing, and it is cheap in code. Keep the previous frame's paint buffer. Inside a mask around the moving figure, multiply the old paint by about 0.85–0.95 rather than clearing it, then lay new strokes on top. The result is real "wiped glass" residue instead of noise spread over the whole frame. The research literature names the failure modes this avoids: frames painted independently produce "pronounced flickering and popping" ([Rosin & Collomosse eds., Springer](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/978-1-4471-4519-6_13)), and strokes that stay put while the subject moves give the "shower door effect" of paint sitting on glass in front of the scene ([Lu et al. 2010](https://gfx.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Lu_2010_IPS/lu_2010_ips.pdf)).

The strokes themselves need to wrap the form. Hertzmann's classic 1998 algorithm paints coarse to fine with several brush sizes. It places long curved strokes that run perpendicular to the colour gradient, and only where the canvas still differs from the target ([SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=116577); [PyPainterly](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pschaldenbrand/PyPainterly/master/README.md)). Litwinowicz added random length, colour and angle jitter for a hand-touched feel, and **clipped strokes to detected edges** so silhouettes stay crisp ([Princeton mirror](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall00/cs597b/papers/litwinowicz97.pdf)). One catch applies to the ghost: inside a flat, glowing cream body there is no colour gradient to follow. The fix is better than image analysis anyway. The code already knows where every bone is, so it can write out a per-frame direction map (along each limb, curving round the joints, radiating from the heart). Strokes can then be anchored to the body in bone coordinates, so they travel with the limbs and never "swim". That is the same idea as Litwinowicz's optical-flow stroke transport, but with zero error.

Realism then comes from the brush. Each stroke can be drawn as a bundle of thin bristle lines, each with slight colour jitter, a taper and its own "runs out of paint" point for dry-brush gaps. At the same time, each stroke adds to a height map that is lit by one fixed light, which gives impasto ridges catching the light along the cream body. Canvas grain stays fixed across frames, because in real frame-by-frame painting the surface is constant while the paint changes. For washes and the ember aura, Tyler Hobbs' method of stacking 30–100 deformed polygons at about 4% opacity ([Hobbs](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/a-guide-to-simulating-watercolor-paint-with-generative-art)) makes glow look like pigment rather than a blur. The MIT-licensed p5.brush library (v3.0.0) packages watercolour fills, bristle brushes and flow fields. Its fills are seeded, so the same seed keeps them stable across frames ([npm p5.brush](https://registry.npmjs.org/p5.brush)). It needs WebGL2, which this container provides, but its speed under software rendering has not been tested.

Boil should be designed, not random. Re-roll each stroke's jitter from a hash of its id and the frame number divided by 2–3. Replace only 20–40% of strokes per "boil tick", fading them in and out (an opacity idea from [Hays & Essa](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~hays/papers/IVBPA_Final.pdf)). Make big brushes boil less than fine detail, and repaint the background plate only every 6–9 frames or crossfade between three pre-painted versions. These cadences are practitioner-style guesses; no source measured boil rates in professional work.

Measured in this container (4 cores, numpy), stamping 20,000 strokes of 12 dabs took 2.0 s and a structure-tensor orientation field took about 1 s per 1080×1920 frame. Full stroke painting therefore fits a budget of about 1.5–3 s per frame, and four parallel workers bring 270 frames to a few minutes. The oil-paint anisotropic Kuwahara filter ([Kyprianidis et al. 2009](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v28i7pp1955-1963)) is the standard "painterly flattening" filter and ships in Blender's compositor ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/sl/latest/compositing/types/creative/kuwahara.html)). Even the basic version was slow under software WebGL here: **1.3 s, 2.5 s and 3.7 s per frame** at radius 4, 6 and 8. It also erases the stroke boil, so use it, if at all, at half resolution on the held background only.

## The heart should be real light that stays red

The second-largest visual gain is treating the heart as a light source in a high-dynamic-range pipeline. A canvas shadow-blur glow clips at white. An HDR pipeline instead blooms over several octaves, tone-maps its highlights, and can cast rays and wrap light onto the paint around it. Both main routes were benchmarked in this container's headless Chrome 153 with SwiftShader software graphics. three.js (MIT) plus pmndrs `postprocessing` (Zlib), running god rays, mipmap bloom, chromatic aberration, noise, vignette and AgX tone mapping, took **about 1.0 s per 1080×1920 frame**. A hand-written WebGL2 chain (6-level bloom, 64-sample god rays at half resolution, ACES curve, grain) took **about 0.45–0.56 s** in roughly 150 lines. SwiftShader here supports 16-bit float render targets, so HDR works. pmndrs itself recommends half-float buffers and a single tone map at the end of the chain ([pmndrs/postprocessing](https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing)).

Three details matter for this scene. Volumetric rays follow the GPU Gems 3 method of summing samples along each pixel's path toward the light in an occlusion buffer ([GPU Gems 3 ch. 13](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-ii-light-and-shadows/chapter-13-volumetric-light-scattering-post-process)). If the ghost is drawn black and the heart white in that buffer, rays spill out of the chest and past the body's edges. pmndrs' ready-made god-rays effect only occludes against real 3D geometry, so a custom mask pass is needed. **Light wrap**, the compositing trick of blurring light and screening it onto the edges of nearby layers ([Foundry Nuke](https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/draw_nodes/lightwrap.html)), makes the heart visibly light the charcoal swirls, picking up their brush grain, instead of floating on top. Finally, keep the red red. In the local test an HDR red of (6, 0.25, 0.15) turned **pink-white at the core under AgX**, while its halo stayed red. Tone curves desaturate bright colours by design ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/cant-get-outer-glow-to-work-with-unrealbloompass/52643)). Keep the core at about 1.2–2.5× in red only, let bloom carry the energy, and use the Neutral operator or a curve applied to brightness only.

ffmpeg is the robust fallback and the right finishing tool. A float-precision glow, colour-shift, vignette and temporal-grain chain ran at **about 0.23 s per frame** here. Glow done in ffmpeg works on already-clipped 8-bit frames, though, so the heart cannot keep more energy than white, and it has no occlusion-aware rays. Use ffmpeg after WebGL for the grade, using a LUT generated in numpy so no third-party LUT licence is involved, plus grain, vignette and the encode. One operational risk is real but manageable. Chromium removed the automatic SwiftShader fallback and now requires `--enable-unsafe-swiftshader` for WebGL without a GPU ([blink-dev](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/yhFguWS_3pM)). If a future Chrome drops even that opt-in, a numpy bloom, tone-map and grain path measured at about 0.46 s per frame works as a fallback.

## Your own acted take beats any mocap library

The current walk is driven by sine waves. Research on emotional gait explains why that cannot carry sadness on its own. Roether et al. motion-captured sad, happy, angry and fearful walks and found that the emotion is not explained by speed alone. It lives mainly in **arm and upper-body joint-angle amplitudes**, with each emotion depending on only a few joints ([Tübingen](https://www.compsens.uni-tuebingen.de/compsens/index.php/people/project/19); [Frontiers review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.842433/pdf)). Libraries also lack this story's hardest moments: straightening up, hurrying after someone, knees giving way, getting up, walking on.

The best path is for you to act all six beats as one continuous side-view take. Put the iPhone on a tripod at hip height, perpendicular to your path, 3–4 m away, with your whole body in frame, in fitted contrasting clothes, at 30 or 60 fps. Claude can run Google's BlazePose model inside the container: the `@mediapipe/pose` npm package bundles the model files, `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` is Apache-2.0, and its model files download from storage.googleapis.com, which is reachable. The output is then smoothed with the One Euro filter (`1eurofilter`, BSD-3) ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/1eurofilter)). Side views are the hard case: far-side limbs are hidden and the left and right legs swap identity where they cross. The clean-up is to trust the near-side limbs, mirror them half a cycle later for the far side, and lock the feet during ground contact. You own the performance, so there is no data licence question. Pose-estimation speed in this container was not benchmarked, but it runs offline, so even several hundred milliseconds per frame is fine.

If you would rather not film, there is a fallback that needs no action from you. The **Bandai Namco Research Motion Dataset 1** has walks in sad, tired, not-confident, old, happy and proud styles as BVH files that Claude can fetch directly ([GitHub](https://github.com/BandaiNamcoResearchInc/Bandai-Namco-Research-Motiondataset)). It is licensed **CC BY-NC 4.0**, so it is unsafe if the reel promotes a brand, client or paid work. In that case use it only as timing reference. The CMU motion-capture BVH conversion is described by its maintainer as free for any project ([cgspeed group](https://groups.google.com/g/cgspeed/c/DSAjQI2iqWM)), though a Hugging Face copy tags it CC BY 4.0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ckinpdx/CMUMDB)). It has few emotional walks. 100STYLE is CC BY 4.0 with 100 locomotion styles ([project page](https://ianxmason.github.io/100style)), and Mixamo is royalty-free for films ([Adobe FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/au/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html)); both require you to download files on the Mac. Phone mocap apps give cleaner 3D than 2D pose estimation. Move One exports FBX and BVH from a single iPhone ([Move One docs](https://docs.move.ai/knowledge/move-one-pricing)), and Rokoko Vision's free tier allows **30 s per month**, exactly one reel, with BVH export on paid tiers only ([Rokoko support](https://support.rokoko.com/hc/en-us/articles/15712580191761-Rokoko-Studio-Subscriptions-plans-explained)). Blender 4.5 is best kept for hand-fixing joins between clips or rendering silhouette and depth passes; for this film it costs more of your time than it returns.

Whatever the source, procedural craft goes on top, because no human capture contains ghost cloth. Damped springs on the head, forearms and cloth make them trail the body by 1–3 frames. Analytic two-bone IK keeps feet planted. Straightening up should run hips, then chest, then neck, then head, with a small overshoot. The collapse needs a tiny anticipation breath, a fast drop and a settle. Light smoothing before reducing to 9 fps matters, because 9 fps exaggerates pops. These rules are standard animation craft rather than sourced findings; the commonly cited walk beat is one step per half second ([summary of *The Animator's Survival Kit*](https://s184753.blogspot.com/2019/01/more-research-animators-survival-kit.html)), which is only 4–5 drawings at 9 fps, so each key pose has to count.

## Colour, camera, sound and type do the emotional work

A colour script, Pixar's practice of fixing dominant colours for each story beat in advance ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-color-script-definition/); [TED Ideas](https://ideas.ted.com/how-color-helps-a-movie-tell-its-story/)), turns the heart into the film's only motivated light. A proposed arc follows; the exact colours are design choices, not sourced values. From 0–2 s, a black frame and one red heartbeat flash with a push-in already moving, as a hook. From 2–8 s, while he hides, the heart is dim and deep red, the ambient light cool, and he is small in the lower third under heavy dark space. From 8–16 s, during the chase, the heart warms to gold, its light spills onto the swirls and the flecks brighten. From 16–22 s, as the family vanishes, the frame desaturates almost to monochrome and the camera stops or pulls back. From 22–30 s, a faint warm ember remains and the final frame matches the opening, so the reel loops. Slow push-ins mark realisations and states of mind ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/camera-shots/camera-movements/push-in-shot/)). Lead room ahead of his run keeps the motion directional ([ExpertPhotography](https://expertphotography.com/lead-room-principle-photography/)). Pushing into depth with layered swirl parallax feels more physical than a flat zoom. Avoid hard cuts and video cross-dissolves. Painted films transform one surface into the next, so transitions should be smears along his running direction, scrape-reveals, or the family breaking into golden strokes that drift away and become the background's flecks.

Sound is where small effort buys the most perceived production value. A heartbeat must be a two-part "lub-dub" or it sounds like a drum. Muffled and low-passed, it reads as heard from inside the body ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/tension-heartbeat-sound-effects)). Lock its timing to the visual heart, so the light pulses on the beat: slow and muffled while he hides, faster and brighter in the chase, irregular at the vanish, then **true silence for 1.5–2 s**. Walter Murch deliberately builds silence into his films because audiences fill it with their own feelings ([16:9 interview](https://16-9.dk/2018/09/interview-with-walter-murch/); [Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2011/11/29/silence)). A single convolution reverb shared by everything, built from exponentially decaying noise, sounds "surprisingly good" ([Vicanek](https://www.vicanek.de/articles/ReverbAlgo.pdf); [reverbGen](https://github.com/jipodine/reverbGen)) and glues the mix into one space. Master to about **−14 LUFS integrated and −1 dBTP**, which is common Reels practice rather than an official Instagram spec ([Soundplate](https://soundplate.com/why-music-sounds-quiet-on-instagram-reels/)).

For text, use an OFL handwritten font such as Caveat or Caveat Brush ([Font Squirrel](https://www.fontsquirrel.com/license/caveat)) or Patrick Hand ([Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/patrick-hand/about)), in warm cream, revealed along a brush path at the boil rate. Meta publishes no official safe zone. Third-party guides block roughly the top 210–250 px, bottom 280–350 px and right 84–90 px of the 1080×1920 frame ([AdaptlyPost](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide); [ScreenSnap](https://www.screensnap.pro/blog/instagram-reels-size-guide)). Keep text roughly within y = 260–1560 px and x = 60–990 px, and keep the heart and face between 35% and 65% of frame height. Reels retention is judged heavily on the first three seconds ([Later](https://later.com/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm/)), which is why the hook beat opens on the heart.

## Technical build: how Claude assembles it in this container

This section is for the coding agent. All stages share one source-agnostic format: a 2D joint JSON at 9 fps with per-frame foot-contact flags. Filmed video, BVH and procedural motion all feed the same painter.

**Motion stage.** Extract frames from your iPhone take with ffmpeg. Serve `node_modules/@mediapipe/...` from a local Node static server, because the CDNs unpkg and jsDelivr are blocked here. Run BlazePose full or heavy in headless Chromium (CPU/WASM), then apply the One Euro filter (mincutoff about 1.0, beta 0.01–0.1), near-side mirroring and foot locking. Retarget by bone angle onto the ghost's own bone lengths, and resample to 9 fps. For BVH, a roughly 100-line numpy forward-kinematics parser is safer than three.js's `BVHLoader`, because Bandai files carry six channels on every joint in ZXY order, which the loader was not tested against. Then add springs on the head, forearms and cloth, plus IK foot planting.

**Paint stage (numpy, or Canvas2D in Chromium; 4 workers).** Each frame emits colour, the figure mask, limb id and depth, a direction buffer (limb tangents, plus curl noise for the swirls) and an emissive buffer. Paint the background plate in three variants with Hobbs-style flow-field bristle strokes plus flecks, held or crossfaded every 6–9 frames. Paint the figure with body-anchored, coarse-to-fine bristle strokes (radii about 48/24/12/5 px), clipped to a dilated limb mask, using seeded partial boil every 2–3 frames and a persistent-canvas residue multiply of 0.85–0.95 in the motion mask. Accumulate the height map, light it from the top left, then apply static canvas grain. Write a clean reimplementation rather than using PyPainterly, which is GPL-3 and needs OpenCV, not installed here. Treat Acerola's Kuwahara shader as reference only, since its licence is unconfirmed.

**Light stage (WebGL2 in Chromium).** Launch with `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`, and confirm that `WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` reports SwiftShader and that the float-buffer extensions are present. Upload the layers as textures and convert sRGB to linear. Build the occlusion mask (heart white, body black), run half-resolution radial god rays (48–64 samples, decay 0.95–0.97), dual-filter bloom in RGBA16F, and light wrap. Finish with a hue-preserving or Neutral tone map. Read frames back with `toDataURL` or `readPixels`, which cost about 0.2–0.3 s; `locator.screenshot` was about five times slower.

**Finish stage (ffmpeg 6.1.1).** Convert 9 fps to the delivery rate, then apply `lut3d` with a numpy-generated grade, `noise=allf=t` grain after the rate conversion so grain changes on every output frame, a subtle `vignette` and `rgbashift`, and x264 at CRF 16–18 with `-tune grain` and yuv420p. For audio, synthesise in numpy (heartbeat pairs at about 50–60 Hz with a pitch drop, noise footsteps synced to foot contacts, band-passed pink-noise wind, a sparse modal-synth piano motif), convolve with the shared IR, and run two-pass `loudnorm` to I = −14 and TP = −1, exported as 48 kHz AAC.

**Budget and risks.** At about 2–3 s of paint plus about 0.7 s of light and readback per frame, 270 frames take roughly 10–20 minutes on 4 cores. Benchmark one frame of each stage before committing, because SwiftShader timings scale with core count and were measured here on 4 vCPU. The open risks are: untested p5.brush speed under software WebGL; side-view pose errors, which call for the filming tips above; Bandai's non-commercial licence; the Chrome SwiftShader opt-in, with numpy as the fallback; and EbSynth, if ever used, which is public domain code but implements Adobe's patented PatchMatch ([GitHub](https://github.com/jamriska/ebsynth)).

## Conclusion

The reel's quality ceiling is set less by rendering power than by imitating the physical process. Painted animation looks hand-made because paint persists and only motion is repainted, and because a real body performed the motion. Both are free to reproduce here: the code already knows where every bone and stroke is, so it can do in bookkeeping what Petrov did with his palms. For this project, human input is worth more than any library or filter. Thirty minutes of you acting the beats, plus your judgement on boil rate and residue, will move the result further than any extra shader.

A practical way to work is to build in the ranked order and render a 3-second test after each step, starting with the persistent canvas and the filmed motion. Those two changes alone should make the ghost read as painted and alive. Glow, sound and grade then polish a film that already works. A useful next experiment is to render the same 3 seconds with boil cadences of 2, 3 and 4 frames and compare them side by side on the iPhone. No source fixes the right rate, so the right one is the one your eye picks.
